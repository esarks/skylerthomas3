**The Woman Who Had No Right to Touch**

Twelve years.

Twelve years of bleeding. Twelve years of being ceremonially unclean. Twelve years of isolation.

Under Jewish law, her condition made her untouchable. Literally. Anyone who touched her became unclean. Anything she sat on became unclean. She couldn't go to temple, couldn't participate in community life, couldn't even brush against someone in a crowd without defiling them.

She'd spent everything she had on doctors. Got worse, not better. Broke. Desperate. A social and religious outcast.

And now there's a crowd pressing around Jesus—the rabbi everyone's talking about. The healer. The one who touches lepers and they're cleansed. The one who doesn't seem to care about the religious rules that keep people like her at a distance.

She has no right to be in this crowd. She's unclean. She could defile everyone around her, including Jesus Himself.

But she's desperate. And desperation makes you bold.

A thought forms: *If I just touch his clothes, I will be healed.*

Not a theology. Not a formula. Just desperate, undeserving faith reaching for what it has no right to ask for.

She presses through the crowd. Hands trembling. Heart pounding. She doesn't ask permission. She can't earn this. She just... reaches.

Her fingers brush the edge of His cloak.

Immediately, she feels it. The bleeding stops. Twelve years of suffering—gone in an instant. Healed. Made whole.

But Jesus stops walking.

"Who touched me?" He asks.

The disciples are confused: "Master, the people are crowding and pressing against you."

But Jesus knows: "Someone touched me; I know that power has gone out from me."

She's caught. Exposed. Trembling, she comes forward and falls at His feet.

This is the moment of reckoning. She's made Him unclean by touching Him. She had no right. She overstepped. Now everyone knows.

She tells Him the whole truth. The twelve years. The desperation. The touch. The healing.

And here's where grace gets scandalous:

Jesus doesn't rebuke her. Doesn't call her out for defiling Him. Doesn't lecture her about breaking religious law.

Instead, He calls her **"Daughter."**

Not "woman." Not "you unclean one." **Daughter.** Family. Belonging.

"Daughter, your faith has healed you. Go in peace and be freed from your suffering."

Read that again: **Your faith has healed you.**

Not your worthiness. Not your religious observance. Not your cleanliness or your goodness or your right to ask.

Your *faith*—your desperate, undeserving, reaching-out-anyway faith.

---

**This is amazing grace.**

She had no right to touch Him. She was unclean by every religious standard. Broke. Desperate. Defiled. A wretch, by any religious measuring stick.

But grace doesn't wait for you to get clean. Grace doesn't wait for you to earn the right to reach out.

Grace meets you in your desperation and says: *"Daughter, you're healed. Go in peace."*

She couldn't earn it. Couldn't deserve it. Couldn't make herself worthy enough to ask.

All she could do was reach out in desperate faith and receive what was freely given.

That's the scandal we've been avoiding: You don't have to deserve grace to receive it. You just have to open your hands.

She reached for the hem of His garment—undeserving, unclean, with no right to touch—and she was made whole.

What if you stopped trying to earn it and just... reached?

What if you let yourself be the desperate one, the broke one, the wretch—and discovered that's exactly who grace is for?

Because here's the truth that makes grace amazing: It's not for people who are mostly good and stumbled slightly. It's for people who are bleeding out, broke, desperate, and have absolutely no right to ask.

And when you reach anyway—not because you've earned it, but because you're desperate—you discover grace doesn't recoil.

Grace stops. Grace turns. Grace looks at you with love and says: "Daughter. Son. You're healed. Go in peace."

Not because you deserved it.

Because grace is scandalously, gloriously, amazingly free.

**Why This Story Fits Chapter 6 Better Than Woman at the Well:**

1. **"Wretch" Theology:** Chapter 6 emphasizes "Amazing grace... that saved a wretch like me." The bleeding woman is ceremonially unclean, isolated, broke—a wretch by religious standards. Fits perfectly.

2. **Undeserved Grace:** Chapter 6's theme is grace you can't earn. The woman has NO RIGHT to touch Jesus (she could defile Him), but she reaches anyway. Perfect illustration of receiving what you can't deserve.

3. **Receiving vs. Earning:** Chapter 6 asks "Can you let go of trying to deserve it?" The bleeding woman can't earn healing, can't make herself worthy—she just reaches in desperation. Exact theme match.

4. **Faith Not Worthiness:** "Your faith has healed you" (not your worthiness, cleanliness, or right to ask) directly supports Chapter 6's theology.

5. **Scandalous Grace:** Chapter 6 calls grace "scandalous"—given to those who don't deserve it. The bleeding woman making Jesus unclean and being called "Daughter" anyway is the scandal of grace personified.
